Chapter 07 · Sections 15-17

Library, Collections, Ratings & Progress

Favourites, saved projects, recently played, custom collections, achievements, XP and leaderboards — with no pay-to-win and no seasonal competitions.

7.1 Library surfaces

Surface Behaviour
Favourites One row per user/project. Toggling is idempotent and updates the public favourite count atomically.
Saved projects A bookmark separate from favourite: "save for later" without signalling interest publicly.
Recently played Last 30 played projects with timestamps; private by default; clearable. Only recorded from validated play sessions.
Achievements Earned badges and progress toward next tiers.
Collections User-curated lists, public or private.

7.2 Collections

  • Create, rename, edit description, reorder items, delete.
  • Visibility: private (default) or public. A public collection is shareable by link and discoverable in search.
  • Add/remove projects; duplicates within one collection are prevented by a unique constraint, not by client checks.
  • Item ordering is explicit (position), reorderable by drag or keyboard controls.
  • Public collections show creator, item count, follower count and like count. Others can follow and like a public collection.
  • Deleting a collection deletes its items and cascades to follows and likes.
  • When a user makes a collection private, existing followers lose access immediately; no notification is sent.
  • Content moderation applies to collection title and description; reported collections can be hidden with the owner notified of the reason.

Example collections: Favourite Games, To Play Later, Best FNF Mods.

7.3 Ratings and reviews

  • One rating per account per project, 1–5 stars, editable, deletable.
  • The average and count are materialised and recalculated on write; a rating change updates both rather than inserting a new aggregate.
  • A written comment/review is optional and independent of the rating. Comments support replies (two levels), likes and reports.
  • Creators can reply once per top-level comment, marked as a creator reply.
  • Anti-brigading: a rating from an account younger than 24 hours, or from an account with no play session on a game, is recorded but weighted 0 in the displayed average until the account ages past the threshold. Weighting is transparent: the UI states when a rating set is adjusted.
  • Moderated or deleted comments are replaced with a tombstone; the rating itself is unaffected unless the account is under enforcement.
  • Accounts deleted by the user have their ratings removed; suspended accounts' ratings are excluded from display while enforcement is active and restored automatically if the suspension is lifted with all penalties cleared.

Audit rule: aggregates never change silently. Each recalculation driven by moderation or enforcement writes an audit record (project, old value, new value, reason, actor, timestamp). Historical aggregates are never rewritten without such a record.

7.4 XP, levels and achievements

Achievement families: Explorer, Player, Collector, Critic, Creator/Contributor, Veteran. Tiers: Bronze, Silver, Gold, Diamond.

Example achievements:

Achievement Family Trigger
First Steps Explorer Launch any project.
Category Cartographer Explorer Play one game in five categories.
Marathon Player 10 hours of measured play.
Curator Collector Create a public collection with 10 items.
Fair Judge Critic Rate 10 projects with written reviews.
Shipped It Creator/Contributor A project you own passes validation.
Founding Duck Veteran Account age of one year with activity in the last 90 days.

Rules:

  • Achievement XP is awarded once per achievement, enforced by a unique constraint on (user_id, achievement_id).
  • XP is written through an append-only ledger; balances and levels are derived or materialised from the ledger, never set directly by a client.
  • Daily caps per family (for example, Explorer activity caps at 150 XP/day) prevent grinding.
  • Self-dealing is blocked: a creator does not earn Creator XP from their own repeated play, ratings or collections.
  • Suspended or banned accounts earn nothing during enforcement and are excluded from leaderboard snapshots.
  • Rewards are cosmetic and recognition-based: badges, profile frames, titles. No gameplay advantage, no purchases, no loot.

7.5 Leaderboards

  • Global XP, weekly, monthly, level ranking, and a contributor ranking based on validated, published project activity.
  • Weekly and monthly boards are rolling windows, not seasons: there is no reset ceremony, no seasonal badge, no "season pass".
  • Contributor ranking does not create a staff role. It measures published work, not permissions.
  • Snapshots are materialised on a schedule (hourly for global, daily for weekly/monthly) so reads are cheap.
  • Ties break by earliest achievement of the score, then by account age.
  • Banned accounts are removed from all boards at the next snapshot; the removal is logged.

7.6 Anti-abuse summary

Risk Mitigation
XP farming Per-family daily caps, diminishing returns on repeats, unique achievement grants.
Bot plays Session fingerprints, minimum session duration for XP eligibility, anomaly detection on play volume.
Rating brigading New-account weighting, one rating per account, burst detection on a project.
Follower inflation Follows from accounts younger than 24 hours are recorded but excluded from public counts until aged.
Duplicate rewards Unique grants plus idempotency keys on every award endpoint.
Leaderboard manipulation Server-side scoring only, immutable ledger, audit of adjustments.

7.7 Data implications

New tables: favourites, saved_projects, recently_played, collections, collection_items, collection_follows, collection_likes, ratings, comments, comment_likes, achievements, user_achievements, xp_ledger, leaderboard_snapshots. Each is specified with keys, constraints and RLS expectations in Chapter 10.
